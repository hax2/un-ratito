# Chapter catalogue and authoring blueprint

## How chapters work

A chapter teaches **one useful communicative focus** through several games. A learner can browse by level, situation, or grammar term: “What was happening?” should also appear in search for “past continuous” and “estaba + gerundio.” All chapters are directly accessible. Suggested prerequisites open a short refresher rather than a locked gate.

Choose difficulty inside the chapter: **Supported** provides meanings and phrase chunks; **Standard** reduces cues and changes contexts; **Stretch** asks for more independent production and subtle interpretation. These are assistance settings, separate from a chapter's language level. Making an A1 sentence faster does not make it advanced.

A chapter contains a 30-second explanation with two audio examples, six narrowly defined learning targets, practice prompts, a few short conversations, and a transfer challenge. One session practises a small part; completing the chapter may take many visits. Let learners skip the explanation and return to it from feedback.

Each target should describe something assessable: “describe an ongoing past activity when another event occurred,” not simply “know the imperfect.” English provides meaning and explanations; Spanish remains central. Use Spain vocabulary and reviewed Spain Spanish audio, including vosotros where natural.

## Launch chapter A: Order politely

**Audience:** beginner, with optional register comparisons for experienced learners.

**Goal:** order a drink, ask for an item, and request the bill in a café in Spain.

Six targets: request one item; add a polite phrase; specify a quantity; specify a modifier; request the bill; recognize/use `quería…` as a softened request in this situation. Teach the latter as a useful chunk first, not a complete explanation of the imperfect tense.

Example Builder prompt: “Ask for a coffee with milk, please.” Canonical answer: `Un café con leche, por favor.` A Tales branch can also accept `Quería un café con leche, por favor.` if supplied as a choice. Neither should be marked wrong because the other is preferred stylistically.

Three story settings: counter order, table order, paying. Audio should make the meaning clear without requiring exaggerated slow speech; offer replay and a slower recording where available.

## Launch chapter B: What was happening?

**Search names:** past continuous, past progressive, estaba + gerundio.

**Goal:** describe an activity in progress at a past reference point, often when another event occurred.

**Teach:** `Estaba cocinando cuando llamaste.` = “I was cooking when you called.” The ongoing activity is viewed from inside it; the call is the other event. `Estar + gerundio` is a progressive construction, and it can appear in different tenses. Do not teach it as the only way Spanish expresses English “was doing.” RAE discusses its progressive role in [the grammar's treatment of gerund periphrases](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica/sintaxis/per%C3%ADfrasis-de-gerundio-ii-el-auxiliar-ir).

### Six targets

1. Say what I/he/she was doing at a past moment.
2. Ask what someone was doing: `¿Qué estabas haciendo?`
3. Describe a group activity, including `estábamos` and `estabais` in suitable contexts.
4. Connect ongoing activity with an interrupting event: `…cuando sonó el teléfono`.
5. Understand two simultaneous activities: `Mientras yo estaba cocinando, él estaba poniendo la mesa.`
6. Distinguish a habit from an activity in progress, while recognizing that simple imperfect and progressive can overlap in valid contexts.

### Concrete game challenges

| Game | Prompt | Answer / feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builder | “Use estaba + gerundio: I was studying when you called.” | `Estaba estudiando cuando llamaste.` Explain the two event roles. |
| Tales | Your friend asks why you didn't answer the phone. Goal: explain you were driving. | `Estaba conduciendo.` Follow with a natural response rather than another conjugation question. |
| Listening | Hear `Cuando llegué, estaban cenando.` Choose the matching event order. | Dinner was already in progress when the speaker arrived. |
| Slip | Task explicitly requests the progressive construction; sentence: `Estaba cocinar cuando llamaste.` | Repair `cocinar` to `cocinando`. |

### Difficulty and accepted answers

Supported: provide the whole auxiliary+gerund chunk and English help. Standard: build both clauses and recognize subject changes. Stretch: infer event relationships from short native-speed conversations and produce a paraphrase with reduced cues.

`Cocinaba cuando llamaste` can be a valid expression. A task specifically practising the progressive should respond “That works; now try it with estaba + gerundio” and record construction practice as incomplete, not label the Spanish ungrammatical. Add a `valid-off-target` assessment outcome for this distinction.

Habitual prompt: “Every Sunday, we ate at my grandmother's.” Prefer an authored habitual-imperfect answer; do not teach the false rule that every English past continuous maps mechanically to the progressive or every `cuando` demands the same tense pairing.

Three launch stories: missed phone call, arriving at a friend's house, explaining a noisy background. Each contains three to five short turns, with scored decisions focused on this chapter.

## Launch chapter C: If I had more time…

**Search names:** imperfect subjunctive, second conditional, si tuviera, si pudiera.

**Goal:** discuss an imagined present or future situation and its consequence.

**Teach:** `Si tuviera más tiempo, aprendería a tocar la guitarra.` = “If I had more time, I would learn to play the guitar.” Teach the high-use pattern as a meaning-bearing pair: `si + imperfect subjunctive` and a conditional consequence. Do not describe the imperfect subjunctive simply as past time.

### Six targets

1. Imagine having a resource: `Si tuviera más tiempo/dinero…`
2. Imagine being able to act: `Si pudiera…`
3. Imagine a different place or situation: `Si estuviera…`
4. Give perspective-based advice: `Si fuera tú…`
5. Ask someone about a hypothetical choice: `¿Qué harías si…?`
6. Distinguish an open condition (`Si tengo tiempo, iré`) from a more hypothetical framing (`Si tuviera tiempo, iría`) using supplied context.

### Concrete game challenges

| Game | Prompt | Answer / feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builder | You lack time. “If I had more time, I would travel more.” | `Si tuviera más tiempo, viajaría más.` Accept reviewed `tuviese` alternative. |
| Tales | Your friend asks where you'd live if you could choose. Goal: imagine living near the sea. | `Si pudiera elegir, viviría cerca del mar.` Continue with their response. |
| Slip | Target a standard hypothetical condition: `Si tendría tiempo, iría contigo.` | Repair `tendría` to `tuviera` or `tuviese`; explain this conditional-clause pattern. |
| Listening | Hear two differently framed plans and choose which presents the situation as hypothetical. | Explain the contextual contrast rather than assigning a numerical probability to a tense. |

### Difficulty and accepted answers

Supported: choose `tuviera` and a complete consequence chunk. Standard: compose both clauses with subject/person changes. Stretch: select a response that preserves the speaker's stance, paraphrase, or continue a conversation with a new verb and no tiles. Include natural vosotros examples when addressing friends.

The `-ra` and `-se` forms both exist in the imperfect subjunctive; see [RAE conjugation tables](https://www.rae.es/buen-uso-espa%C3%B1ol/conjugaci%C3%B3n-espa%C3%B1ola). Teach `-ra` first for consistency and accept `-se` in these reviewed conditional prompts. This is not a global string replacement rule for every use of a `-ra` form.

Three launch stories: imaginary holiday, choosing where to live, advising a friend. Keep stretch variants available from launch so experienced learners can practise intention and production, not just identify endings.

### Next chapters in the imperfect-subjunctive family

Keep these separate because the communicative purpose changes:

- **Past requests:** `Me pidió que le ayudara.` Focus on what someone asked another person to do.
- **Wishes:** `Ojalá tuviera más tiempo.` Focus on a wish about the current situation.
- **Softened requests:** `Quisiera hacer una reserva.` Explain context/register, and compare natural alternatives.
- **As if:** `Habla como si lo supiera todo.` Focus on comparison and speaker attitude.

Author and review each chapter independently. The distinction between indicative and subjunctive depends on meaning and context; the [RAE guide to mood](https://www.rae.es/libro-estilo-lengua-espa%C3%B1ola/el-modo-indicativo-o-subjuntivo) illustrates why blanket trigger-word rules are insufficient.

## Transfer and chapter progress

Show: targets introduced, targets successfully practised, and targets recalled later in a fresh context. A chapter's transfer challenge uses unseen prompt wording with known vocabulary. It cannot be the same sentence with its answer shuffled.

At higher levels, include both selecting an appropriate interpretation and producing a response. Tile success alone cannot establish independent production. Optional typing should have a proper phone keyboard and accent assistance; speaking aloud may be offered for practice, but without automatic pronunciation scoring.

## Authoring checklist

For every chapter record: ID, title, grammar search aliases, approximate level, communicative goal, six target IDs, prerequisite refreshers, supported games, explanation, Spain locale, review status, and audio coverage. For each prompt record its intended meaning, acceptable alternatives, off-target-but-valid answers, distractor rationale, assistance level, and exactly what evidence it can provide.

Use a Spain Spanish reviewer for naturalness and audio direction. A structurally valid JSON pack is not proof of good teaching. The examples in this planning document are seeds for that review, not a substitute for it.
